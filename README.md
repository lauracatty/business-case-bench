# Business Case Bench

Business Case Bench is built around one argument: most AI business cases do not fail because the model cannot work; they fail because the adoption assumption was never stress-tested.

The tool takes an AI idea from definition to readiness, value model and one-page business case. Its headline is not the total score or a polished ROI number. Its headline is the assumption most likely to break the case.

The bench is deliberately opinionated: it asks for EU AI Act classification before money, treats the weakest readiness dimension as the adoption risk, and turns sensitivity into a sponsor challenge rather than a decorative chart.

## Why ranges beat point estimates

A single ROI figure looks precise, but it usually hides the argument. AI value depends on behaviour: how often people use the tool, how much time it really saves, whether the work volume is stable, and whether managers reinforce the new process.

Ranges make uncertainty visible. A pessimistic, expected and optimistic view does not pretend to remove risk. It shows where the case survives uncertainty and where it collapses.

## Why sensitivity is the headline

The most useful business-case question is not "what is the expected benefit?" It is "which assumption would change the answer most if we are wrong?"

That is why Business Case Bench ranks sensitivity across adoption, time saved, volume, minutes per case, hourly cost and licence cost. The largest swing is highlighted because that is where the sponsor should ask for evidence before funding the case.

The improved version also adds an adoption break-test: the minimum adoption needed to hit an 18-month payback. That changes the conversation from "do we like the ROI?" to "can we prove enough people will actually use it?"

## Why only half of time saved is counted

Time saved is not automatically cash saved. Standard Forrester TEI practice commonly counts only a portion of productivity improvement as realisable benefit, then applies a risk adjustment to reflect uncertainty.

The default 50% productivity recapture is deliberately conservative. It assumes some saved time leaks into context switching, review, backlog growth or work that is valuable but not directly monetisable.

## Why this is capacity released, not headcount removed

The model treats time saved as capacity released because that is how many AI cases create value in practice. Faster case assembly can reduce queues, absorb demand, improve quality, shorten cycle times and free specialists for higher-value work.

It does not assume jobs disappear. That would usually overstate the benefit, create avoidable resistance, and confuse operational value with workforce reduction. The better test is whether the organisation can turn released capacity into a measurable service outcome.

## Why there is a decision gate

A positive number is not always an approval. If the use case is prohibited, the right decision is to stop. If readiness is weak, the right decision is to fix the blocker before scale. If the case is financially positive but high-risk, the right decision is to proceed only with controls, oversight and evidence.

That is the point of the bench: the business case should name the next management action, not just calculate a benefit.
